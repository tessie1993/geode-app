# core/audio/ingest

The ring is here; `core/sound/hop` reads each live ring on a thread of its own.

**Owns:** one native PCM ring per audio source (the player tap, the mic, another app's audio, the wallpaper's output-mix capture; later a decoded file for export). Each write carries the sample rate and the source's channel count, so a format change can never pair new samples with an old rate. Converts int16 and unsigned 8-bit to float here, not in Kotlin.

**Here now**
- `PcmRing.{hpp,cpp}` (`geode::audio::ingest`): the lock-free single-writer ring (power-of-two capacity, a runway a slow reader stays behind, an atomic head and epoch), the writers for interleaved float, int16 and raw bytes in an encoding, and `Cursor`, the each-sample-once reader. A take ends where the rate changes, so one take has one rate. Readers hold the ring const and never change it, so a second reader (an output callback) can read the same samples.
- `TrackFeed.{hpp,cpp}` and `AiffReader.{hpp,cpp}`: an export's track audio. `TrackPump.{hpp,cpp}` steps the decode (`android/TrackDecoder.cpp`: AMediaExtractor and AMediaCodec over the descriptor the shell opened, or the AIFF reader for what the extractor does not read) and pushes each chunk; the feed converts it to float, drops what precedes the range start, and hands the export renderer's engine exactly the frames up to each video frame (`geode_track_feed_*`, `geode_aiff_*`, `geode_viz_hear_track`). The soundtrack transcoder (`core/output/AacTranscode`) reads AIFF through the same reader (`openAiff`).
- `LayerMix.{hpp,cpp}`: a second ring layered over a reader's own (the mic over the player's tap, `geode_pcm_ring_set_layer`). Each renderer mixes at its reader: the layer waits in a short queue (0.1 s to join, 0.25 s at most), is resampled to the block it joins and added, so the player's samples stay the clock; once the player has stopped for 0.3 s the layer is handed out alone. The mic's level is added as it is: on the phone's speaker it hears the track a second time, quieter and a room later.
- `SnapshotStitch.{hpp,cpp}`: a ring marked as a capture's snapshots (`geode_pcm_ring_set_snapshots`; the wallpaper's output mix, ~21 ms of every 50 ms) has each write stretched to the real time since the last one, filled from that snapshot alone by overlap-added grains, so readers get real time with no seam. `snapshot_stitch_test.cpp` proves the clock, the level, and the tempo of click tracks heard that way.
- `CapturePlan.{hpp,cpp}`: the formats the mic and other apps' capture try, in order (the device rate, then 48, 44.1 and 22.05 kHz; stereo before mono for other apps' audio; float before 16-bit), the AudioRecord buffer (4 minimums, at least one 1024-frame read) and the read size (`geode_capture_*` in `core/api/geode_capture_api.cpp`, `capture_plan_test.cpp`).
- `MicInput.{hpp,cpp}`: live input, the mic as a device input stream whose callback writes the mic's ring (no JVM thread, no lock or allocation in the callback). The mono plan of `CapturePlan`, the UNPROCESSED source where the device has it, else VOICE_RECOGNITION; a disconnect reopens the stream, at most 3 times in 10 s (`ReopenBudget`). The stream is behind `MicStreams`: `android/OboeMic.cpp` (an Oboe input stream, shared, normal latency) on the device, a fake in `mic_input_test.cpp`. The C ABI is `geode_mic_*` in `core/api/geode_mic_api.cpp`.
- `CapturePump.{hpp,cpp}`: the captures Android has only as Java objects (another app's audio, an `AudioRecord` over a MediaProjection; the wallpaper's output mix, a `Visualizer`), built through JNI (the shell hands down only the MediaProjection, or just start and stop), read on a native thread of their own, written straight into a ring with the capture's silence (`sound/Silence`), and released natively. The reader is behind `CaptureReader`: `android/JavaCapture.cpp` (the format plan tried against `AudioRecord.Builder`, the `Visualizer` set up on session 0, the thread attached to the VM, `AudioRecord.read` into a direct buffer, `getWaveForm` polled) on the device, a fake in `capture_pump_test.cpp`. The C ABI is `geode_java_capture_*` in `core/api/geode_capture_java_api.{h,cpp}`.
- The C ABI is `geode_pcm_ring_*` and `geode_pcm_cursor_*` in `core/api/geode_ingest_api.cpp`; `geode_viz_feed_ring` (`core/api/geode_viz_api.cpp`) lets a live renderer take from a ring with its own cursor, so no sample crosses JNI on the GL thread.
- `RingKinds.hpp`: the rings the app keeps and their sizes. The player's and the mic's (layered over it) are made on first use and kept for the process (`geode_pcm_ring_shared`), so a writer thread, a stage or a surface that still holds the handle never reaches a freed one; the output mix's is one per capture, stitched, made by `geode_pcm_ring_create_kind` and freed by its owner. `pcm_ring_kinds_test.cpp` pins them.
- Lifetime: a `geode_pcm_ring` handle and every cursor made from it share the ring, which is freed with the last of them; a renderer's feed and Cast's audio pump (`core/output/CastAudioPump`) keep the ring on their own.

**Contract**
- In: interleaved PCM from the tap's direct `ByteBuffer` (read where it lies), the mic's input stream, or a capture's native read thread, one writer at a time.
- Out: what each reader's cursor has not seen yet, with its rate. Live, `core/sound/hop/AnalysisThread` reads it on a thread of its own, one per ring, and renderers read the published hops (`core/sound/hop/SharedAnalysis.hpp`); an export runs the analysis inline. The raw tail the renderer needs is the mono mix of what the renderer's engine heard (`core/viz/PcmHandoff`), so `pushPcm` is gone.
- Clock: the sample count. No wall time, except where a snapshot ring's write lands on it (the capture clock, `SnapshotStitch`).

**Moved in**
- `SampleRing.kt` and `SoundCursor.kt` (gone: the shell holds the handle), `SoundInput.kt` and `PcmSink.kt` (gone: the rate was a separate volatile beside the ring, now it is in each write)
- the per-sample loops of `PcmTap.kt` (16-bit and float from the tap's buffer), `AudioCapturePump.kt` (int16 to float), `OutputMixCapture.kt` (8-bit to float), Cast's float to int16 (`CastStream.kt`, now `geode_pcm_cursor_take_s16`) and the wallpaper's level snapshot (`SoundSource.kt`, now `geode_pcm_ring_heard_at`)
- the decode loop, per-sample loops and pre-roll cut of `render/offscreen/TrackAudio.kt` (gone) and the AIFF header walk and sample conversion of `dev.geode.audio.AiffPcm` (gone: the soundtrack transcoder opens its reader natively, `core/output/AacTranscode`)
- the peak scan of `MicCapture.kt` and `PlaybackCapture.kt`: a write returns the block's peak
- the rate, channel and encoding ladders and the buffer size of `MicCapture.kt` and `PlaybackCapture.kt` (`CapturePlan`)
- the mic's AudioRecord, its read thread and its source pick (`MicCapture.kt`, the mic path of `AudioCapturePump.kt`; now `MicInput`)
- the read thread and silence timing of `AudioCapturePump.kt` (gone), the `AudioRecord` construction of `PlaybackCapture.kt` and the `Visualizer` setup and listener of `OutputMixCapture.kt` (now `CapturePump` and `android/JavaCapture`)
- ~~`app/.../audio/PcmRingBuffer.kt`~~ gone earlier (#486)

A cursor behind the player's sink takes with a limit (`Cursor::take(limit)`): the frame the speaker
plays now, from the playout clock (`core/sound/interp/SpeakerSync.hpp`), so the picture does not lead the
sound by the sink's buffer.

**Still to move:** nothing here; `app/.../audio/dsp/NativeDspProcessor.kt` has no sample loop (it forwards each block to `GeodeNative.dspProcessInto`).

**Tests that prove it:** `core/tests/track_feed_test.cpp` (TrackFeed and AiffReader against the Kotlin they replaced), `core/tests/track_pump_test.cpp` (the decode and hand-out order), `core/tests/mic_input_test.cpp` (live input over a fake stream: the ladder, the preset, the writes, stop, disconnects), `core/tests/capture_pump_test.cpp` (a capture's read thread over a fake reader: the writes, a read error, stop, silence), `core/tests/layer_mix_test.cpp` (the layer: two rates, a silent mic, mic on and off mid-stream, pause and resume, epochs, drift) and `core/tests/pcm_ring_test.cpp`, which writes PCM in random chunk sizes with rate changes mid-stream, on one thread and with a writer thread, and checks the reader sees every sample once, in order, each with the rate it was written at; it also holds the old Kotlin `SoundCursorTest` cases, the encodings and the C ABI.
