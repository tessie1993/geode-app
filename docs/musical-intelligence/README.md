# Musical intelligence database

What the app knows about Western pitch, scales and modes, and how it turns that knowledge into visual
decisions. The tables are generated by `tools/musical_intelligence.py` and are not edited by hand. The same
script writes `core/sound/ScaleTable.inc`, the copy the sound engine reads, so the docs and the app cannot
drift apart. To add or retune a scale, edit `SCALES` in the script and run it.

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| [`notes-hz.csv`](notes-hz.csv) | Every equal-tempered note from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (E0 20.60 Hz … D#10 19 912 Hz, 120 notes), A4 = 440 Hz: MIDI number, sharp and flat names, octave, frequency, pitch class, period |
| [`scales.csv`](scales.csv) | 46 scales and modes: semitones from the root, step pattern, degrees (1 b2 2 …), interval names, brightness, tension, feeling, where it is used |
| [`scales-all-roots.csv`](scales-all-roots.csv) | Every scale spelled on all 12 roots (552 rows), in sharps and in flats |

## How the notes are tuned

`f = 440 · 2^((midi − 69) / 12)`. Each semitone multiplies the frequency by 2^(1/12) ≈ 1.0595, and each
octave doubles it. Pitch class is `midi mod 12` (C = 0 … B = 11). The app reads the music's chroma from
spectral peaks between 250 Hz and 2 kHz. Below 250 Hz a semitone is narrower than one bin of the
engine's spectrum, and above 2 kHz partials blur the notes together.

## Brightness and tension: how a scale feels

A scale's mood comes mostly from which degrees are raised or lowered against the major scale. Every raised
degree (#4 in Lydian) makes it brighter. Every lowered one (b3, b6, b7, b2, b5) makes it darker. This
gives the classic ordering of the church modes from brightest to darkest (Persichetti, *Twentieth-Century
Harmony*):

**Lydian → Ionian → Mixolydian → Dorian → Aeolian → Phrygian → Locrian**

Each step to the right lowers one more degree. Tension comes from the unstable intervals a scale contains:
the tritone, the minor second (b2 against the root), and the augmented second (b6 to 7 in harmonic minor).
Pentatonic scales have neither, which is why they never sound wrong.

The `brightness` (-1 … 1) and `tension` (0 … 1) columns are **design values for the visuals**, ranked by
those two rules. They are not measured emotions. How a mode "feels" is partly learned in Western music
culture (Huron, *Sweet Anticipation*, 2006; Parncutt, "The emotional connotations of major versus minor
tonality", *Psychology of Music*, 2014), so treat the feelings as a common reading, not a law.

## How the app uses it

The sound engine (`core/sound/Harmony`) checks every scale on all 12 roots (552 candidates) against
the slow (~4 s) chroma, four times a second. The chroma is first capped at twice its mean, so one loud
note cannot outvote the others, and spectral peaks that are overtones of a stronger note are discounted.
Each candidate is scored by the Pearson correlation of the chroma with the scale's notes, plus a pull
toward a strong root and fifth. That pull is what tells apart modes that share one set of notes (C Ionian
and A Aeolian). A better fit has to hold for 2 s
before the reading changes, so one borrowed chord does not flip the mood. The sound frame then carries:

| Field | Meaning | What it can drive |
|---|---|---|
| `scale` | the catalogue index (`scales.csv` order), -1 before any music | a look per scale family |
| `scaleRoot` | the root's pitch class / 12 | the base hue (one colour per key) |
| `scaleBrightness` | 0 darkest … 1 brightest, gliding over ~1 s | palette warmth, exposure, saturation |
| `scaleTension` | 0 calm … 1 tense, gliding over ~1 s | jitter, distortion, sharp against round shapes |
| `scaleFit` | 0 atonal or noise … 1 clearly in the scale | how much the visuals trust the four above |

These sit beside the key (`keyHue`, `keyMajor`), the chord colour (`chordHue`), the moment-to-moment
chord dissonance (`tension`) and the rhythm and form fields. All of them are described in
`docs/research/musical-layer.md`. `core/tests/musical_test.cpp` plays eight scales as synthetic melodies
over a drone and checks that each is named on the right root.

## The emotion of each key and scale

`tools/music_emotion.py` (run by `musical_intelligence.py`) adds the feeling of each entry:

- `emotions.csv`: all 552 key + scale combinations. Each has a valence, energy and tension, the key's
  sharpness, and the nine GEMS feelings (wonder, transcendence, tenderness, nostalgia, peace, power, joy,
  tension, sadness), with the two strongest named.
- `key-characters.csv`: the 24 keys as Schubart described them in 1806 (Steblin's translation, condensed),
  where Mattheson and Charpentier disagree, how far the three agree, and the GEMS code each became.

The **scale** gives three quarters of each fingerprint. It comes from measured findings: the third, each
lowered degree, semitones, tritones, augmented steps and symmetry. The **key** gives a quarter. Its
character is tradition that listeners cannot hear in equal temperament, so it changes the kind of
feeling and the warmth, never happy against sad.

The app never shows these words. The auto-director (`core/viz/Director`) uses them to choose palette,
motion, camera and what reacts to what, at each section and drop. `docs/research/auto-director.md` has
the sources, the weights and their evidence.
